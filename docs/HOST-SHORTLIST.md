# Host shortlist: the pool the requester can be sent to

**Status: a shortlist, not an approach.** Nothing has been sent to anyone.
**Fetched:** 2026-11-13, from CDAS's published list of approved PLCO projects,
`https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/plco/` filtered to the data and
biospecimen study types.

**Why this list.** CDAS answers the eligibility question with a sentence that
puts the programme's remaining dependency in one place: a professor has to
submit the request, and the requester has to be part of that institution to
have access to the data. The pool of people who can be asked to be that
professor is not a secret — CDAS publishes every approved PLCO project with
its Principal Investigator and institution, and the investigators who have
been through the biospecimen review already hold the biosketches and know
what the review asks.

**What is in it, and what is not.** These are the twenty most recently
approved PLCO projects, from 2026-11-06 back to 2026-08-24. The list is
paginated, and CDAS's pagination and keyword filter both require a session
this machine does not hold, so the extract is the first page rather than all
1,735. The names are Principal Investigators of *approved* projects; the
request type of each — data-only, EEMS, PAR — is on the project's own page
and is not in the table below.

**The chosen first recipient, in full.** Alex Bryant is **Alex K Bryant,
MD, MAS** — Assistant Professor in the Department of Radiation Oncology at the
University of Michigan, staff physician and research investigator at the VA
Ann Arbor Healthcare System, and a radiation oncologist practising
genitourinary and thoracic radiation oncology. His stated research interests
are "precision screening for lung and prostate cancer", cancer epidemiology,
oncology data science, and microsimulation of screening and diagnosis. CDAS
lists him as the Principal Investigator of PLCO-2077 with the contact
address bralex@med.umich.edu; the university's own page gives
bralex@umich.edu.

**Three things in it are worth noticing.**

- **A company can be the Principal Investigator.** Five of these twenty are
  companies: Wholomics GmbH, Pictor Labs, AIRA Matrix, Artera Inc. and
  InfotechSoft. CDAS says the applicant must be "employed by a private
  company, a academic or research institution capable of executing that
  MTA", so a company is a permitted counterparty, and companies are already
  carrying PLCO projects. The request does not have to be pitched to an
  academic department.
- **The closest precedent is a pilot, and it is a company's.** The
  Wholomics project is "Analytical Suitability and Storage-Stability Pilot
  for a Serum NMR-Based Multi-Omics Multi-Cancer Early Detection Assay Using
  Pre-Diagnostic PLCO Specimens" — a new assay, validated for analytical
  suitability and storage stability, on pre-diagnostic specimens. That is
  the first step this programme needs too, and PLCO's own policies require it
  of any new assay. NCI's own most recent PLCO project is a pilot too:
  "PILOT 96 samples from prior HCC study to Nomic".
- **The requester's own address is on this list.** "Regents of the
  University of Michigan" appears as an approved PLCO institution
  (PLCO-2077). The requester's e-mail address is at Michigan and he studied
  there, so the institution where CDAS wants him to hold a standing is one he
  already has a connection to and an address at.

## The order, and the choice

The first letter goes to **Alex Bryant, Regents of the University of
Michigan** (PLCO-2077, "Estimating prostate cancer screening, diagnostic
follow-up, and detection processes for a natural-history microsimulation
model", approved 2026-09-08).

The reasoning is about the shape of the ask rather than the biology of the
recipient's own project, which is prostate screening modelling and not lung
cancer. Three things line up:

1. **The institution is the one CDAS names.** CDAS requires the requester to
   be "part of the institution" that submits, and the University of Michigan
   is where the requester already has an address and has studied. It is the
   standing he can most plausibly be given without a job, and the letter can
   say why he is writing to that person at all.
2. **The machinery is live there.** Bryant holds an approved PLCO project,
   so the review is not new to the institution and the investigator already
   has the biosketch.
3. **The subject is the programme's own question.** Detection processes in a
   screening cohort — when a signal becomes visible and how early — is what
   the programme's simulator answers and what the assay is for.

**And a second Michigan PI, whose subject is closer.** The sweep of the full
list found **Brian Denton, Ph.D., "The University of Michigan", Position
Title Professor, PLCO-2057, approved Jul 14 2026**, "Adaptive Diagnostic
Testing Algorithms for Design of Cost-sensitive Composite Biomarker Tests" —
on composite tests, on the cost of early detection, on several biomarkers
that "no single biomarker is satisfactory on its own". That is the
programme's own proposal: a composite test of two ion channels, on plasma,
at a stated cost. **Denton is the better subject match; Bryant is the
better-documented contact.**

**A correction to the record.** The data-access document said the list "can
be filtered to biospecimen studies". It cannot: the biospecimen-only filter,
the filter the document's own URL carries, and no filter at all all return
the same 1,735 projects and the same first page. The list is therefore every
approved PLCO project, not a list of investigators who have been through the
biospecimen review, and the request type of a project is on that project's
own page rather than in the list. The shortlist above is consequently a list
of PLCO investigators, some of whom have used specimens and some of whom
have not.

**Two more things the project pages say, which the list does not.**

- **Being the Principal Investigator of an approved PLCO project is the
  evidence of eligibility, and the list is enough to establish it.** Every
  candidate on it has had a PLCO proposal approved with them as the named
  PI, which means CDAS has already accepted them in that role on the data
  route — whatever the rank requirement turns out to be. That is a better
  argument than the one this document used to make, and it is one the record
  can stand on: the list is a list of PIs, not a list of people who have
  been through the biospecimen review.
- **The PAR pilot and the EEMS application are two steps, not one.** The
  Wholomics project's own summary says that the case-control validation
  study, including a formal assay reproducibility assessment, is "to be
  submitted as a separate EEMS application". So a PAR pilot is compatible
  with a later EEMS application by the same investigator, and the
  prohibition on submitting to both at once is a question about sequencing
  rather than a bar. That is what message F asks.

**What this document does not do.** It does not settle who would answer an
e-mail from a researcher they have never met, and it does not settle whether
the laboratory the programme needs exists at that institution at all. Those
are the reasons the ranking below is about the *shape* of the ask rather
than about scientific fit. It also does not treat these twenty as the
universe: they are the twenty most recent, and the full list is larger than
the record previously said — see the correction below.

**A correction to the record: the pagination works.** This document used to
say that CDAS's "pagination and keyword filter both require a session this
machine does not hold, so the extract is the first page rather than all
1,735". That was wrong: it was a URL-form error. The working form is

```
https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/
  ?data_types=plco_data&data_types=plco_data_images
  &bio_types=plco_bio_eems&bio_types=plco_bio_par&p=N
```

Verified 2026-11-13: `p=1` and `p=2` return different rows, and the table
reports "1-20 of 1735". The `?study=plco` and `/approved-projects/plco/?p=N`
forms both drop the page parameter, which is what produced the wrong
conclusion. **The universe of PLCO PIs is 1,735, not 20**, and it can now be
swept. The first sweep found a second University of Michigan PI whose subject
matter is closer to the programme's than Bryant's — Brian Denton, PLCO-2057,
"Adaptive Diagnostic Testing Algorithms for Design of Cost-sensitive
Composite Biomarker Tests" — and the routes by which the requester could be
"part of the institution" are worked out in
[`HOSTING-ROUTES.md`](HOSTING-ROUTES.md).
