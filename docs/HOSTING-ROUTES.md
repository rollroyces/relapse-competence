# The routes by which the requester could be "part of the institution"

**Status: researched and recorded 2026-11-13.** This document answers one
question — how could a researcher with no appointment, no employer and no
funding satisfy CDAS's requirement that the requester be "part of the
institution" — and it answers it from CDAS's own published record and from
the institutions' own published policies. Nothing here has been sent to
anybody.

**Why this document exists.** CDAS's reply of 2026-11-12 was that "you will
need to find a professor to submit this data request and you must be part of
the institution to be able to have access to CDAS data". The programme's
reading, recorded before this document, was that the only way to satisfy the
second clause was to be adopted by an institution: an address is not enough,
an appointment or a sponsorship is. That reading drove the host invitation at
[`HOST-INVITATION.md`](HOST-INVITATION.md), which asks one professor to carry
both the proposal and the requester's standing.

**The reading was too narrow, and CDAS's own approved-project list says so.**
Three kinds of Principal Investigator on that list are in, or close to, the
requester's position, and each was approved. The routes below are therefore
not a search for a host; they are four shapes of answer, three of which the
requester can reach without asking anyone's favour.

---

## 1. The decisive finding: CDAS has already approved PIs in the requester's position

These are not inferences. Each is a project page on CDAS, read on
2026-11-13, and each shows the two fields that decide the question: the
**Institution** the PI gives, and the **Position Title** they give.

**A self-employed consultant, with her own consultancy as the institution.**

> Principal Investigator Name: Deborah Goldwasser. Degrees: PhD. Institution: **Statistical Consultant**. Position Title: **Statistical Consultant**. Project ID: NLST-43. Initial CDAS Request Approval: Dec 3, 2013. Title: *Size-Driven Simulation Model for Lung Cancer Screening*.

The summary is in the first person: "I am the sole-author of a novel
mathematical methodology which allows for the estimation of a size-dependent
natural history model of lung cancer from cancer registry data…"
(<https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/613/>). There is no university
in the Institution field, and CDAS approved the project.

**A visiting researcher, on a PLCO project, on lung-cancer screening.**

> Principal Investigator Name: Hilary Robbins. Degrees: PhD, MHS, MSPH. Institution: **Queen Mary University of London**. Position Title: **Visiting researcher**. Project ID: **PLCO-380**. Initial CDAS Request Approval: Jul 5, 2018. Title: *Validation of Liverpool Lung Project (LLP) Model Version 3*.

The summary: "We plan to use PLCO data to validate our model and examine
possible risk thresholds for selecting ever-smokers into lung screening"
(<https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/1882/>).

**A company's chief executive, on a PLCO project.**

> Principal Investigator Name: Patrick Strasser. Degrees: M.Sc. Institution: **Wholomics GmbH**. Position Title: **CEO**. Project ID: 2026-0185. Initial CDAS Request Approval: Sep 15, 2026.

(<https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/6246/>)

**What this establishes.** The written rule — a PI who is "a permanent
employee of your institution at a level equivalent to, but not limited to,
that of an academic professor … or senior researcher" — is applied to the
*record*, and the record contains a sole trader, a visiting researcher and a
chief executive. The written rule also says "You must provide your
institution and its location", and in one case the institution is a
self-description. **A full sweep of the approved-project index by the
researcher's agent reported 197 projects with a corporate PI institution
across 137 distinct corporate entities, and 17 corporate PIs who give a
personal Gmail or Yahoo address as their institutional e-mail**; CDAS
publishes no approved-user list, so the individual cases in this section were
re-read rather than taken from the sweep, and the sweep's counts are recorded
as reported rather than re-verified.

**What it does not establish.** These approvals were granted between 2013
and 2026, and CDAS's eligibility desk may apply the written rule more
strictly now than it did in 2013. Nothing in the public record says how the
desk reads "part of the institution" today, which is why message G is still
worth sending.

## 2. Route A — be the institution

If the requester incorporates a consultancy, the "Institution" field can
carry it, and he is his own permanent employee and his own senior
researcher. The precedent is NLST-43 above, where the institution field
carried a self-description and the PI was the sole author of the work.

This route needs nobody's permission, which is its whole appeal: it is the
only one of the four that does not depend on a stranger reading an e-mail
and saying yes. It has two real costs. The "senior researcher" test has to
be argued rather than assumed, and it is argued on a publication record
rather than on a job title. And it does not by itself satisfy the germline
route (§5).

## 3. Route B — a visiting appointment at a university

The precedent for this shape is PLCO-380 above. The question is which
institutions have a published mechanism, and what each requires. The
candidates are the institutions already holding approved PLCO projects, so
that one person can carry both the affiliation and the proposal.

**The two quotations that carry the best routes.** Florida State: "A courtesy
appointment is a designation in the University's HR application system (OMNI)
that does not include compensation or confer employee status on the
appointee", and it "permits the individual to access university privileges,
such as FSU email accounts, computer system access, departmental shared
drives, or building access" (<https://hr.fsu.edu/sections/employee-data-management/courtesy-appointments>).
Florida Atlantic's live research-misconduct policy names the category and
shows it covers research: the policy applies to anyone "affiliated by
contract or agreement with Florida Atlantic University, including any
courtesy (unpaid) appointments, also known as Affiliate or Voluntary
appointments", and to "the operation of tissue and data banks and the
dissemination of research information"
(<https://fau.edu/research-admin/research-integrity/research-misconduct/>).

**A correction to the record.** The Provost's policy PDF that the research
cited for Florida Atlantic — the one listing "Obtain a University e-mail
address" among the privileges of an affiliate appointment — **now returns 404**. The category itself is confirmed by live pages (above), but the
privileges list is not, and it is not cited here as though it were.

**Two institutions with no PLCO project, mentioned only because they are
the shape of the answer.** New York University's A/P/A Institute grants the
title of Visiting Scholar "with or without academic appointments at other
institutions", and the only third-party ask is a "Letter of recommendation
from an academic colleague or advisor" rather than an institutional
sponsor; Texas Tech's OP 74.07 covers scholars who visit "to pursue various
research activities without formal (salaried) affiliation". Neither holds an
approved PLCO project, so neither solves the proposal.

## 4. Route C — the company route

CDAS permits a company to be the counterparty, and the programme's own
record says so: the reply to the biospecimen route was "An MTA is required
so we would need to be employed by a private company, a academic or research
institution capable of executing that MTA". The approved-project list bears
this out — 197 projects have a corporate PI institution, across 137 distinct
corporate entities, in Austria, France, the United Kingdom, Singapore, India,
Australia, Japan, Germany and Israel as well as the United States.

**And one of those companies is doing the requester's experiment.**

> Principal Investigator Name: Patrick Strasser, M.Sc. Institution: **Wholomics GmbH**. Position Title: **CEO**. Project ID: 2026-0185. Initial CDAS Request Approval: Sep 15, 2026. Title: *Analytical Suitability and Storage-Stability Pilot for a Serum NMR-Based Multi-Omics Multi-Cancer Early Detection Assay Using Pre-Diagnostic PLCO Specimens*.

Its stated motivation names the same three constraints the requester's assay
specification addresses: "current approaches face limitations in
**early-stage sensitivity, specimen volume requirements, and cost**". Its
design is the requester's design: "Before requesting irreplaceable PLCO
specimens for a full retrospective validation study, the suitability of
long-term archived PLCO serum for this assay must be established. We propose
a small analytical pilot using **100 PLCO serum specimens: 50 incident
solid-tumor cases (lung, colorectal, ovarian, bladder) and 50 matched
cancer-free controls**, split equally between specimens stored at **−70 °C
(T0–T2) and −157 °C (T4/T5)**." Its first aim is to "Determine whether
long-term archived PLCO serum yields spectral quality and signal recovery
sufficient for the assay … characterizing any evidence of analyte degradation
associated with archival storage", and its second to "Compare assay readouts
between specimens stored at −70 °C (T0–T2) and −157 °C (T4/T5), using a
balanced 25/25 case/control allocation per storage stratum".

That is the Taizhou letter's question — *can this assay be measured on this
archive at all, and does what is recoverable decay with the age of the
sample* — asked on PLCO rather than Taizhou, by a company whose PI is its
chief executive.

Wholomics is small and has an inbound route. Its own site gives "Our
partners are fundamental to our success … **Become a Partner** — If you would
like to partner with us, fill out the form below and we will get back to
you", and its team page lists four people, the first of whom is "Patrick
Strasser, MSc Co-Founder, CEO — Cancer Research, Strategy, Operations,
Fundraising, **Partnerships**", with offices in "Boston, USA | Ried i. I.,
Austria" and a US telephone number (+1 617 222 0055). Its assay is "currently
for Research Use Only".

**Why a company is the only route that satisfies everything.** CDAS needs
affiliation; the germline route needs employment; the biospecimen route
needs an MTA counterparty. A company that employs the requester satisfies
all three at once, and the records above show that CDAS accepts a chief
executive and a sole trader as a PI, and a company as the institution.

## 5. The asymmetry that decides the route: affiliation is not employment

The PLCO EEMS Policies and Procedures, §10, on the germline route:

> "Any bona fide research group may apply through dbGaP … This application includes verification by the institution that **the investigators are full time employees**, the IT system meets specific standards for the protection of sensitive data, any posted embargo will be respected, and the research will conform to standard protections of privacy and confidentiality."

(<https://cdas.cancer.gov/files/download/l0g32brjto/policies_procedures_plco.pdf>,
p. 15, read 2026-11-13.)

So the programme has two different identity requirements. The **clinical-data
route** may be satisfied by an affiliation, because that is what CDAS's
approved-project record shows. The **germline route** — the dbGaP half of
the per-molecule attribution — requires the institution to verify that the
investigator is a full-time employee, and **no sponsorship, courtesy
appointment or covered-visitor status satisfies that**. An affiliation route
leaves the germline half of the attribution unresolved; an employment route
resolves it.

This is the finding that reorders the routes. It was not in the programme's
record before this document, and it is the reason Route C is not merely
another option.

## 6. The University of Michigan route, which is still real and now has two candidates

SPG 601.42 remains the best-documented mechanism, and it remains the only
one whose eligible categories name the requester's own category: "a U-M
alum". The evidence is in
[`UM-AFFILIATION-MECHANISM.md`](UM-AFFILIATION-MECHANISM.md).

**But UM has two approved PLCO investigators, and the second one is a
better subject match than the first.**

> Principal Investigator Name: Brian Denton. Degrees: Ph.D. Institution: **The University of Michigan**. Position Title: **Professor**. Project ID: **PLCO-2057**. Initial CDAS Request Approval: Jul 14, 2026. Title: *Adaptive Diagnostic Testing Algorithms for Design of Cost-sensitive Composite Biomarker Tests.

Its summary: "Since no single biomarker is satisfactory on its own, attention
is turning to ways to combine biomarkers into composite tests. There are many
open questions about which tests to use, when to use them, and how to combine
them to screen for diseases in a way that balances the benefits of early
detection with the potential costs of testing". That is the requester's
proposal in one paragraph — a composite test of two ion channels, on plasma,
at a stated cost, for early detection of a drug-tolerant state.

Alex Bryant (PLCO-2077, prostate screening microsimulation) was chosen
because the *institution* lines up with the requester's own address. Brian
Denton lines up on the institution **and** on the subject. Neither has a
published e-mail address on their CDAS page; Bryant's is recorded in
[`HOST-SHORTLIST.md`](HOST-SHORTLIST.md) as `bralex@med.umich.edu` (CDAS) and
`bralex@umich.edu` (the university's own page).

## 7. A correction to the record: CDAS's pagination works

The programme's record says CDAS's "pagination and keyword filter both
require a session this machine does not hold, so the extract is the first page
rather than all 1,735". **That was wrong: it was a URL-form error, not a
session problem.** The working form is:

```
https://cdas.cancer.gov/approved-projects/
  ?data_types=plco_data&data_types=plco_data_images
  &bio_types=plco_bio_eems&bio_types=plco_bio_par&p=N
```

Verified on 2026-11-13: `p=1` and `p=2` return different rows (the second
page runs from Aug 10, 2026 back to Jul 14, 2026), and the table reports
"1-20 of 1735". The `?study=plco` and `/approved-projects/plco/?p=N` forms
both drop the page parameter. The consequence is that the twenty-name
shortlist in [`HOST-SHORTLIST.md`](HOST-SHORTLIST.md) is twenty of 1,735, not
the universe, and the universe can now be swept.

## 8. What this document does not establish

- **It does not establish that CDAS would approve the requester today.** The
  precedents are from 2013 to 2026, and the eligibility desk may apply the
  written rule more strictly now.
- **It does not establish that a visiting appointment satisfies CDAS.** It
  establishes that a visiting researcher was once an approved PI on a PLCO
  project. Whether CDAS's *current* desk reads it that way is question G.
- **It does not establish that any institution would actually extend an
  appointment to a researcher with no current post.** The policies are
  permissive in wording; the practice is unit by unit and unpublished.
- **It does not establish that Wholomics would want the requester.** Their
  assay is a commercial MCED product and the requester's is a transcript
  readout of a drug-tolerant state; they are adjacent, not identical, and
  what they would want in return for it is unknown.
- **It does not establish a processing time for anything.** No institution
  in this document publishes one.
- **It does not establish that the requester qualifies as a "senior
  researcher"** for the purposes of the PI rule, which is a judgement about
  a publication record and not a policy question.

## 9. The order the programme should work in

**The company route first, and the reason is subject matter rather than
politics.** Wholomics is running the requester's own experiment — an
analytical-suitability pilot on archived pre-diagnostic specimens, with
matched controls, split by storage temperature — on the same resource, and
its chief executive is the named PI on it. A four-person company with an
inbound "Become a Partner" form and a CEO whose remit includes Partnerships
is a much more answerable recipient than a professor with two hundred
e-mails a week, and the letter to it is not a cold approach: it is a
proposal to do the same thing with a different analyte.

**The University of Michigan route in parallel, through Brian Denton rather
than Alex Bryant**, because the institution is the one where the requester
already has an address and where SPG 601.42 names his category, and because
Denton is working on composite biomarker tests and the cost of early
detection, which is the requester's subject.

**Message G to CDAS in parallel with both**, because it is the question that
decides whether an affiliation is enough on its own. If the answer is that a
sponsorship or a courtesy appointment will do, the University route is
sufficient for the clinical data. If the answer is that employment is
required, only the company route survives.

**And the Taizhou route remains the one that needs no institution at all.**
It is blocked on the requester sending
[`TAIZHOU-COLLABORATION-REQUEST.md`](TAIZHOU-COLLABORATION-REQUEST.md), and
the Wholomics project is evidence that the ten-aliquot feasibility pilot that
letter asks for is the right shape of first step, because a company that has
just proposed exactly that pilot on PLCO is the obvious candidate to do it.
