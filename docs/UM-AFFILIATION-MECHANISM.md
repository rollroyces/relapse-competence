# How a researcher with no appointment gets institutional standing at the University of Michigan

**Status: researched and recorded 2026-11-13.** Every quotation below was read
from the page it is attributed to, on that date, with one exception noted in
§5. Nothing here has been submitted, requested or filed with the University;
this is a reading of published policy, and the ask that follows from it is in
[`HOST-INVITATION.md`](HOST-INVITATION.md).

**Why this document exists.** The CDAS reply of 2026-11-12 was flat: "you will
need to find a professor to submit this data request and **you must be part of
the institution** to be able to have access to CDAS data." The requester holds
an address at the University of Michigan, where he studied, and holds no
appointment anywhere. Until 2026-11-13 the programme's understanding was that
the cheapest route to "part of the institution" was a departmental arrangement
of some kind, and that its shape was unknown — which is why
[`HOST-INVITATION.md`](HOST-INVITATION.md) asked the host a question instead of
proposing a route.

This document answers that question from the University's own published
policies. **The answer is that the University already has a mechanism, it is
free, it is departmental rather than employment, and it names the requester's
own category — a U-M alumnus — as eligible.**

---

## 1. The finding

**The University of Michigan operates a university-wide, non-employment
affiliation mechanism for exactly this situation, and it is written down.**

`SPG 601.42, Covered Visitors` is the governing standard practice guide. It
was issued 4 November 2025 and last updated 5 November 2025. Its definition
of who qualifies is the operative text, and it does three things at once: it
covers people who **do not** hold a U-M appointment, it excludes U-M
employees, and it names **"a U-M alum"** as an eligible category.

> "An individual is a COVERED VISITOR if the individual is (i) invited or
> permitted by a SPONSORING UNIT, a FACULTY SPONSOR, and/or a STAFF SPONSOR to
> engage with U-M's NON-PUBLIC RESOURCES, whether in-person, remote, or a
> combination, for a period greater than or equal to 14 consecutive calendar
> days, (ii) does not meet the EXCLUSIONS below, and (iii) **does not hold a
> U-M appointment**."
> — <https://spg.umich.edu/policy/601.42>, §III.A.1 (definition)

<!-- SPG CATEGORY QUOTE -->
> "COVERED VISITING AFFILIATE/COLLEAGUE - an individual from industry or government, a staff member at another institution of higher education, **a U-M alum**, or an individual otherwise unaffiliated with another institution of higher education or other organization"
>
> — <https://spg.umich.edu/policy/601.42>, §III.A.1 (categories)
<!-- END SPG CATEGORY QUOTE -->

> "U-M employees, including those appointed in the HR system under SPG
> 201.34-1, individuals holding 'dry' faculty appointments (i.e., 0% effort)
> under SPG 201.34-2, and those granted an emeritus or emerita title by the
> Board of Regents ... are excluded. U-M students are excluded."
> — <https://spg.umich.edu/policy/601.42>, §III.A.2 (exclusions)

So the mechanism is not an employment appointment and not a courtesy title. It
is a third thing: a **sponsored affiliation** with a sponsoring unit, which
the University defines by the *absence* of an appointment. The requester falls
inside the definition on all three counts — a U-M alum, not an employee, not a
student.

Three further properties matter for cost and for time:

- **It is renewable and bounded.** "COVERED VISITORS may engage with U-M for
  up to two (2) years at a time (renewable)." (§IV.B)
- **It is sponsored by a person, not by a committee.** The definition names a
  "FACULTY SPONSOR" and a "STAFF SPONSOR"; the sponsoring unit is "the
  college, school, department, institute, or other unit responsible for a
  COVERED VISITOR". (§III.A.3)
- **It applies to the right campus.** "SPG 601.42: Covered Visitors became
  effective on November 5, 2025. This SPG applies to the Ann Arbor, Dearborn,
  and Flint campuses, and Michigan Medicine."
  — <https://hr.umich.edu/working-u-m/my-employment/academic-human-resources-services/spg-60142-covered-visitors>
  (Michigan Medicine is the relevant one: the chosen host is in Radiation
  Oncology at the University of Michigan.)

The onboarding that a sponsoring unit performs is enumerated in the policy, and
it is short: granting appropriate access to building, computer, data systems,
equipment and facilities; finalising the relevant agreements "e.g., Visiting
Scholar/Scientist/Student agreement, letters of invitation, liability waivers,
and non-disclosure agreements, available through the Office of the Vice
President for Research and Innovation"; Restricted Party Screening where
required; required training; and, "If required in the SPONSORING UNIT for
others who are engaged in similar activities, a COVERED VISITOR must complete a
background screening in accordance with SPG 201.95 prior to engaging in any
such activities." (§IV.B.2)

## 2. How the affiliation becomes an identity, and an address

SPG 601.42 establishes the affiliation. The University-wide machinery that
turns it into an account is the **MCommunity Sponsor System**, and it is
documented in the same plain terms.

> "The Sponsor System allows authorized U-M staff members to create online
> identities for university-affiliated individuals who do not receive uniqnames
> and online identities otherwise. Sponsored individuals include conference
> attendees, contractors, incoming faculty, staff, or temporary employees who
> need access to U-M resources before the hiring process is complete, academic
> affiliates, and others."
> — <https://documentation.its.umich.edu/node/255> (Overview of the Sponsor System)

> "The Sponsor System allows units to sponsor people **as members of the U-M community** for specified periods of time. An individual may be sponsored by more than one unit."
> — same page

The affiliation type that matters is named explicitly, and it is the one the
requester's situation fits:

> "The primary purpose of sponsorship is to document an active relationship with the unit, for example **academic affiliate (e.g. research collaborator)**; other university affiliate (e.g. volunteer); and short-term guest (e.g. conference attendee)."
> — same page

And the uniqname type that carries an institutional identity is distinguished
by the people who receive it:

> "**Regular Uniqnames with UMIDs** — These sponsored people receive a regular
> uniqname and UMID, as well as a Wolverine Access/M-Pathways entry. ...
> People receiving regular uniqnames: Incoming Faculty/Staff (who may need a
> uniqname before they complete the hiring process) This includes incoming
> regular and temporary employees. Contractors. **Academic Affiliates.** Other
> University Affiliates."
> — same page

The directory then records the affiliation in the same taxonomy the rest of
the University uses:

> "All current members of the U-M community have profiles in the MCommunity
> Directory. This listing includes faculty, staff, students, retirees, alumni,
> and **sponsored affiliates**."
> — <https://documentation.its.umich.edu/node/256> (Overview of the MCommunity Directory)

> "Sponsored people are entered when a sponsorship administrator enters
> information about them in the MCommunity Sponsor System." — same page

And "member of the U-M community" is not the repository's phrase; it is the
University's, used in the context of granting and removing access:

> "It is part of a larger identity management system that allows the university
> to know who is, and who is not, a member of the U-M community. This enables
> central offices—as well as departments, schools, colleges, and campuses—to
> grant and remove access to their online resources as needed and
> appropriately."
> — same page

**The address follows from the identity.** The University's own services page
lists sponsored affiliates among those eligible for the standard computing
services, which include the account and the mail:

> "Those eligible to receive the services include students, faculty (including
> emeritus faculty), full-time or part-time staff, and **sponsored
> affiliates**."
> — <https://its.umich.edu/computing/computers-software/standard-computing-services>

> "Your U-M Account — Uniqname and UMICH password. ... A profile in the
> MCommunity Directory. ... **Google at U-M** — includes the core apps of
> Gmail, Calendar, and Drive, as well as others."
> — same page

## 3. The part that matters most, and was the surprise

The CDAS requirement is not merely that the requester have an address at the
institution. It is that he be *part of* it — and it is the University's own
research-oversight machinery that says how a non-employee gets that standing.

**U-M's own IRB guidance says a visiting scholar counts as affiliated with
U-M during the term of the sponsorship, and it says a study team member needs
no appointment at all:**

> "Generally, Active Emeritus Faculty, Adjunct Faculty, Visiting Faculty,
> **Visiting Scholars**, Visiting Graduate Students, International Visiting
> Scholars and Volunteers vetted through Volunteer services **count as
> affiliated with U-M during the term of their sponsorship/appointment**.
> Contact your IRB (IRBMED or HSBS) for confirmation."
> — <https://az.research.umich.edu/medschool/guidance/external-non-um-study-team-members/>
> (IRBMED, External (non-UM) Study Team Members; approval date 21 November 2025,
> last updated 4 March 2026)

> "A study team member is not a student and has **no Appointment Department
> selected**" — same page, listed as one of the ways an external collaborator
> is identified.

And the system that records the study team says the same thing about
appointments:

> "The Appointment Dept column of the 1.3 Study Team Members table displays the
> HR appointment for each person listed. The appointment information will
> automatically populate for study team members who meet all of the following
> criteria: Have a U-M affiliation (e.g., employee); Are not a student; Have a
> single (1) appointment."
> — <https://documentation.its.umich.edu/node/1251> (Regulatory Management:
> Adding a Study Team Member)

> "If the value in the Appointment Selection Complete? column is No for a study
> team member, you must select an appointment before submitting the study
> application. ... **N/A = an appointment is either optional or not
> required.**"
> — same page

> "Individuals are added to the Select Person list after they are created
> (i.e., **Sponsored accounts**) or log in to eResearch for the first time
> (i.e., uniqname accounts)."
> — same page

Read together, these three sources say: at the University of Michigan, the
thing that puts a person inside the institution for research purposes is an
**affiliation**, and a sponsored academic affiliation is enough. An HR
appointment is one way to have an affiliation; it is not the way.

**This is the specific point that was missing from the programme's
understanding.** The requester's obstacle was never employment. It was
*institutional membership*, and the University has a documented, no-cost,
non-employment route to membership whose eligible categories include a U-M
alumnus — with the sponsoring role held by the very person the programme is
already writing to.

## 4. What the programme now knows

| Question the programme had | Answer, with its source |
|---|---|
| Can a person with no U-M appointment hold a U-M affiliation? | Yes — SPG 601.42 Covered Visitors, defined to require that the person "does not hold a U-M appointment" |
| Is the requester's own category eligible? | Yes — "a U-M alum" is named in SPG 601.42's COVERED VISITING AFFILIATE/COLLEAGUE category |
| Is it employment? | No — U-M employees are excluded from the definition |
| Who sponsors it? | A faculty or staff sponsor, or the sponsoring unit — SPG 601.42 §III.A.3 |
| What does it cost? | Nothing. It is not employment and carries no salary, stipend or benefits |
| How long does it last? | Up to two years at a time, renewable — SPG 601.42 §IV.B |
| Does it give an institutional identity? | Yes — a regular uniqname with a UMID, a Wolverine Access/M-Pathways entry, and a MCommunity profile listing the affiliation as "Sponsored Affiliate" |
| Does it give an institutional e-mail address? | Yes — sponsored affiliates are eligible for the standard computing services, which include Google at U-M (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) |
| Does it make him "part of the institution" for research-oversight purposes? | Visiting Scholars "count as affiliated with U-M during the term of their sponsorship/appointment" (IRBMED); a study team member may have no Appointment Department at all, and the value is "N/A = an appointment is either optional or not required" (ITS eResearch) |
| Does CDAS accept it? | **Not established.** CDAS's own wording — "users at your institution" — is what has to be read against this. See §5 and message G |

The last row is the one that has not been answered, and it is the reason the
host letter still asks a question. But the shape of the question has changed
completely: it is no longer "what standing would I need?", which has no answer
in the requester's gift, but "**would you sponsor me?**", which is a
department-level request with a named policy behind it.

## 5. What this document does not establish

- **It does not establish that CDAS will accept a sponsored affiliate on the
  approved-users list.** CDAS's rule is "users at your institution" and its
  reply was "you must be part of the institution". Whether a *sponsored
  affiliation* counts as "part of the institution" for CDAS's purposes is a
  question for CDAS, and it is the question in message G. Nothing in U-M's own
  policy can answer it, because U-M's policy governs U-M's internal
  definition of affiliation and does not speak for NCI's.
- **It does not establish that the University's own arrangement is sufficient
  for CDAS.** A sponsored affiliation gives an institutional address, a UMID
  and a directory affiliation. It is a *stronger* answer to "users at your
  institution" than an alumni address alone, because it is an affiliation
  rather than a mailbox, but it is not the same thing as employment and CDAS
  has not been asked.
- **It does not establish that the host will sponsor.** Units "have substantial
  discretion regarding whether to provide a sponsorship for a given individual,
  why, and for what duration" (ITS). The request is a request.
- **It does not establish a processing time.** No U-M page states a processing
  time for a sponsored-affiliation request. The Covered Visitor Central
  Information System "is in development through ITS" (HR), so there is no
  published pipeline to quote.
- **It does not establish that the arrangement is free of work for the host.**
  Onboarding requires agreements to be executed, Restricted Party Screening
  where required, and possibly a background screening under SPG 201.95 "If
  required in the SPONSORING UNIT". The host's department does this work.
- **The Library's "Register as a Researcher" is not a route to any of this.**
  It was checked, because it is easily mistaken for one. It is a Special
  Collections request account: "Students, faculty, and staff: Use your U-M
  credentials. Guests: Register and create an account"
  (<https://www.lib.umich.edu/find-borrow-request/access-special-collections-materials/register-researcher/>).
  It grants no address, no standing and nothing bearing on restricted data.
- **The alumni e-mail address is a mailbox, not a status.** The University
  confirms lifetime forwarding for alumni, and U-M's directory keeps alumni
  profiles for life, but no U-M page calls an alumni address an "institutional
  address" for a restricted-data purpose. It is the *affiliation* that the
  sponsor route supplies, and the address is its by-product.

## 6. The one verification gap

The ITS documentation pages (`documentation.its.umich.edu`) and the Medical
School Office of Research pages were fetched through the in-app browser rather
than by direct request, because a scripted request to those hosts is answered
with an HTTP 403. The text quoted in §2 and §3 was read from the rendered page
in the browser, from the same URLs given, on 2026-11-13. The SPG 601.42 text
in §1 and the HR page text were fetched directly and in full. This is recorded
because a future reader checking these quotations will hit the same 403 and
should know the quotations are from the rendered page, not from a bypass.

## 7. What follows from this, in the programme's order

1. **The host letter asks for a sponsorship, by name.** It no longer asks an
   open question about standing. It names the mechanism — a sponsored
   affiliation for an academic affiliate under SPG 601.42 — and asks the host
   whether their department would sponsor it. The ask is smaller, which is the
   point: a professor asked to sponsor a specific arrangement is being asked
   something a department does routinely.
2. **Message G to CDAS asks the question this document cannot.** Whether a
   sponsored affiliation satisfies "users at your institution" and "part of
   the institution". That question is now worth asking precisely because the
   answer is knowable in the favourable direction.
3. **The fallback stays in the letter.** If no arrangement is possible, the
   analysis is run by someone at the host's institution on the pre-specified
   plan and code, and the programme takes the results. That route needs no
   standing from the requester at all and remains open.
